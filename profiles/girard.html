Profile. Martin Girard is a full-time community advocate and activist whose public work has focused on homelessness, civil liberties, drug policy, human rights, environmental issues and accountability at Victoria City Hall. He describes five years of personal homelessness, three years of frontline volunteering, current residence in supportive housing and experience responding to overdoses with naloxone. Campaign material also describes an honours science education. He has appeared before council and in local reporting on encampment enforcement, SOGI, civil rights and transparency. For voters, Girard offers the field’s strongest lived-experience challenge to enforcement-led homelessness policy; his most consequential proposals also require close scrutiny for municipal authority, legal risk and implementation capacity.

2026 campaign and social verification. martingirardforvictoriacouncil.ca is a current, authorized campaign site with an extensive platform and a frequently updated post archive, including material dated through August. The platform covers homelessness, drug policy, law and order, Indigenous relations and the environment. Girard opposes encampment sweeps and criminalization of poverty, argues for housing and early intervention, supports harm reduction and proposes directing police away from simple-possession enforcement while licensing regulated compassion clubs or storefronts. He also campaigns on disclosure of City bylaw-department investigations and has filed a judicial-review petition concerning an OIPC access decision. Facebook, Instagram and VoteMate are linked directly from the campaign. No other official social channel was identified.

Sources. Campaign site and platform, post archive, 10 Jul judicial-review announcement, VoteMate, campaign-linked social accounts and local coverage collected on the site. Personal history and education are self-reported and should be labelled accordingly.

Current evidence requiring first full score. Direct evidence now exists for M9/M11, M26/M26b, M31b/M33, M58, M67, M70b, M79/M79b and M80, with clear divergence from enforcement-oriented measures.
