Who they are

Lyall Atkinson is a Victoria-born small-business owner and second-time mayoral candidate. His 2022 candidate material states he was born in 1963 at Victoria General Hospital into a large pioneer family, worked in the health and food industries, and then founded Your Friend with a Truck Ltd., a moving and hauling business he has operated for roughly two decades. He is married, lives in Victoria, volunteers in the community and builds classic and hot-rod cars. He has no elected municipal record, so there is no accountability block for him.

Nomination status — why this profile exists. As of 2 Sep 2026 the City's elections page lists exactly two accepted mayoral nominations: ALTO, Marianne and ATKINSON, Lyall (plus GARDINER, Marg for Council). On the City's own record Atkinson is therefore a filed mayoral candidate, while Harris and McGuigan have announced publicly but neither appeared on the accepted-nominations list at the 2 Sep 2026 check. Nominations close 11 Sep 2026 at 4 PM; the official Declaration of Candidates follows shortly after. Treat the accepted-nomination fact as hard, and re-verify the full field on 11 Sep.

Electoral history. Ran for Mayor of Victoria as an independent in 2022, finishing fifth of eight with 175 votes — 0.6% of the 27,182 votes cast for mayor (Alto 15,090; Andrew 9,775; Marshall 1,513; Fuentes 206; Atkinson 175). No other campaigns located. A filed nomination is not a competitive campaign, and the 2022 result is the only performance evidence on file.

2022 positions — vintage, not current, not scored. Recorded verbatim in substance from his 2022 candidate material so the file is complete. Do not treat any of it as a 2026 commitment:

  • Core functions. Council should concentrate on local issues; his 2022 material argued that "drug addiction and homeless are a blight on the city, it is not the sole responsibility of local government to solve these societal issues."
  • Public safety. Reduce crime; let the police chief and bylaw officers do their jobs without political interference (framed in 2022 against the then-mayor).
  • Homelessness. Enforce park camping restrictions on a 7 PM–7 AM basis, close provincially-owned hotels used for sheltering, relocate unhoused people to their city or province of origin, and convert one hotel into a locked recovery facility.
  • Downtown and economy. Revitalize the core by making it more business-friendly, growing tourism and restoring hotel capacity to pre-crisis levels.
  • Taxes and spending. Opposed specific council spending decisions, including Indigenous-fund taxation and grants to board members he characterized as improper.

No letter grades. Consistent with the page rule that a dash is unknown rather than a fail, no ACTW measure is marked for Atkinson. The relocation-of-unhoused-persons position in particular raises Charter and inter-jurisdictional questions that a 2026 campaign would have to restate before it could be assessed.
