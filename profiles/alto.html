Marianne Alto

Profile. Marianne Alto is Victoria’s incumbent mayor and is seeking a second term. A professional facilitator with university degrees in law and science, she owns Azimuth Research & Consulting, specializing in problem solving, change management and facilitation, and lives in Burnside Gorge. First elected to Victoria City Council in 2010, she served 12 years as a councillor before winning the mayoralty in 2022 with 15,090 votes (about 55%). She also served on the Capital Regional District Board from 2011–2018 and returned in 2022. Her current regional roles include the CRD Board, BC Transit Board and BC Urban Mayors’ Caucus; she has chaired the CRD Arts Commission and Performing Arts Facilities Select Committee. She has also held long-standing City Family and reconciliation roles with the Songhees and Esquimalt Nations. For voters, the central fact is experience: by the 2026 election she has 16 years at the council table, including four as mayor, so her candidacy can be assessed against an extensive municipal and regional record.

2026 campaign status and priorities. Alto formally launched her re-election campaign on 20 May 2026 under the slogan “Experience that delivers.” Her three announced priorities are unlocking more homes, taking action on community safety, and investing in community and wellbeing. She has also named transportation, parks, business support, arts and culture, heritage and regional policing as second-term priorities. She says she will not run with a council slate.

Housing evidence — approvals are not completions. Her launch cited more than 7,000 housing units approved during the term. The Times Colonist separately reported 2,359 net new occupancy permits since provincial targets began in 2023—about 48% of the five-year target of 4,902—and 363 below-market rentals over the preceding two years. These figures measure different stages and should not be conflated when assessing delivery.

Safety and wellbeing evidence. Alto presents the Community Safety and Wellbeing Plan as unfinished multi-year work requiring continued funding and intergovernmental partnerships. Launch coverage credited the programme with 97 new living spaces and increased police, bylaw, sanitation and service-provider capacity; treat those as campaign claims pending a published outcome dashboard.

📋 Accountability — what the council did, on the record. Four consecutive years above inflation: 6.15%, 7.93%, 6.99%, and 7.28% aggregate in 2026, which is 9.34% residential and 4.78% business, about $323 more on a median $1,015,000 home. The 2026 budget opened at 13.3% in November and roughly $7M of the reduction came from one-time measures including a cancelled $2M debt-reduction payment and a $2.8M parking-reserve draw — an approach she described herself as not a long-term solution, which is the exact conduct M66c is written to prevent. Operating budget $300.0M adopted 2023 to $394.1M adopted 2026, roughly 31%. Zero modern efficiency tools in four years: no ZBB, no competitive benchmarking, no permit-processing benchmarks, no quarterly dashboard, no municipal AI standard. MNP governance report not implemented despite being on file. Climate Friendly Homes uptake not published. Missing Middle delivered 61 permits in 2024 against a 150 target. CSWB Phase 1 funded by drawing $2.5M from Centennial Square, $4M from the Financial Stability Reserve and $1.5M from Royal Athletic Park. Dowler Place: a confidential draft motion authored by her, to convert Dowler Place into a Pandora relocation site, was disclosed by CHEK via an FOI in which the documents were accidentally attached, June 2025, with consultation skipped because "we knew what the comments would be from the public." She issued a statement: "I recognize that there has been confusion and frustration… residents haven't had a full or accurate picture of the project… I apologize for that confusion." A related item belongs on the record and was missing from this page: firefighter Josh Montgomery was suspended one day for an open letter to Premier Eby opposing the hub; Alto declined to apologise to him and publicly told the Premier that local personnel matters were "not his bailiwick" (Times Colonist, 2024). She has stated no position against any of these measures. This is what a council did across four budgets, and it is the baseline any 2027 council starts from.

⚪ Still. Permits and AI pre-screening, managed competition, the performance dashboard, accessibility and washrooms, streetlighting, GIS and AI governance, the Integrity Commissioner, the regional bill, the Household Bill.

What would move it. A published position on any of the five method measures. Four years of a mayoralty produce Close marks; the method gap is a record, and only a stated commitment closes it.
