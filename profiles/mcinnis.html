Framework/Candidate Profiles/Arthur McInnis
Profile

Arthur McInnis

Council candidate · Victoria 2026

Decision 14B+17

Campaign. arthurmcinnis.ca

Evidence vintage (summary). arthurmcinnis.ca — full read July 30 (materially changed profile)

Web & socials. arthurmcinnis.ca

Sources. arthurmcinnis.ca, read in full July 30 2026 — and that read is the reason this profile changed substantially; Victoria News June 25 2026. More than 30 years as a lawyer, educator and advisor on government, infrastructure and finance projects; has taught law at UVic and in Australia and Hong Kong. James Bay. Five published platform areas: open government and fiscal responsibility; neighbourhood planning and development accountability; housing affordability and tenant protection; transportation; public safety.

✅ Aligned (6), three of them found July 30. An independent integrity commissioner, named directly — the strongest single overlap between the framework and any newcomer's platform (M79b). A real-time taxpayer dashboard for major city projects — narrower in scope than M67 and more demanding in cadence (M67). "Audits of existing city programs and spending," which is M65 in substance (M65). NEW: "streamlining approvals for appropriate infill through tools such as pattern books and pre-approved designs" — M7b named almost verbatim. NEW: "Every emergency doesn't need a police car. Tiered dispatch, community paramedicine, and proper coordination with mental health and addictions services are practical tools that work, and I will push to maintain or implement them" — M28d named directly.

🟢 Close (4), three of them found July 30. Public consultation requirements for tax increases above inflation — structurally the same instrument as M66's supermajority-plus-published-explanation override, arrived at independently (M66). NEW: tiered dispatch plus mental-health coordination against M26b's civilian crisis team. NEW: "greater use of co-operative housing and city-owned land to create homes for working families, seniors, and young people" against M6b. NEW: "we need more Missing Middle housing: townhouses, multiplexes, courtyard housing" — the composition mix M6 targets, which this page previously recorded him as lacking.

🟡 Partial (4). Neighbourhood planning and development accountability as a platform pillar with no participation or survey mechanism (M80). "When they go downtown, they can't find parking, they can't get where they want to go quickly" — precisely what M19 and M24 target, no mechanism (M19, M20b). Downtown diagnosis without a vacancy or licensing mechanism (M71). Affordability as a pillar without a debt rule or utility-inclusive figure (M66d).

One genuine tension, unresolved and stated rather than smoothed. Infrastructure reviews tied to development approvals adds a condition to the approval path where M7 commits to compressing it. Both are accountability instruments; only one is a speed commitment. He has not published his reasoning and it should be asked rather than assumed. An earlier version of this page asserted a motive for it; that was inference and was removed.

📋 Correction history. Two claims about him on this page were this document's, not his: he is a former lawyer and educator, not a sitting UVic professor, and the reasoning attributed to him on infrastructure reviews is not on the record. On the tax number he was closer to the truth than the framework was — he said "10 per cent this year alone" and Victoria News corrected him to 9.34% residential, while the framework was leading with the 7.28% aggregate.

⚪ Still. Managed competition, streetlighting, climate, the regional bill, cleanliness, accessibility, families and schools, and the Foundation measures.
