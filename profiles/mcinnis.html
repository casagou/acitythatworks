Who they are

Arthur McInnis is a James Bay resident, law professor, former international construction and projects lawyer and former tribunal member with more than 30 years of experience examining government, infrastructure, finance and contractual decisions. He has taught law in Canada and abroad and describes four years of close analysis of Victoria planning and governance decisions before entering the race. For voters, his professional strength is institutional accountability: he offers the field’s most detailed legal and procedural programme, although many proposals would still require staff, statutory and financial feasibility review.

One genuine tension, unresolved and stated rather than smoothed. Infrastructure reviews tied to development approvals adds a condition to the approval path where M7 commits to compressing it. Both are accountability instruments; only one is a speed commitment. He has not published his reasoning and it should be asked rather than assumed. An earlier version of this page asserted a motive for it; that was inference and was removed.

Three of his mechanisms are things the Program has already considered and declined, so they are disagreements rather than gaps. His Public Parking Infrastructure Replacement Policy (60–75K dollars per stall into a Parking Infrastructure Reserve, funding parkades at Centennial Square, Wharf Street and Royal Athletic Park), his advocacy for OCP repeal and replacement, and his public integrity reviews of past rezonings are all recorded in the Program's declined-imports list. They cannot score, and saying so is fairer to him than leaving them as silence. Four further mechanisms have no cell in the 55 at all: the free-vote covenant, the single-meeting model, the open-books pro-forma audit before any affordability relaxation, and inner-harbour water transit.
