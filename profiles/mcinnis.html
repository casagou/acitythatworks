Profile. Arthur McInnis is a James Bay resident, law professor, former international construction and projects lawyer and former tribunal member with more than 30 years of experience examining government, infrastructure, finance and contractual decisions. He has taught law in Canada and abroad and describes four years of close analysis of Victoria planning and governance decisions before entering the race. For voters, his professional strength is institutional accountability: he offers the field’s most detailed legal and procedural programme, although many proposals would still require staff, statutory and financial feasibility review.

2026 campaign and social verification. arthurmcinnis.ca is a current, authorized campaign site with a full platform, biography, ten long-form article hubs, donations, volunteering and sign requests. Its five planks are open government and fiscal discipline; neighbourhood planning and responsible growth; housing affordability and tenant protection; transportation for a functional city; and public safety with measurable accountability. Named mechanisms include a real-time taxpayer dashboard, civic land inventory, independent Integrity Commissioner, zero-based budgeting, a 90-day consultation before above-inflation tax increases, binding CALUC consultation, infrastructure-capacity certification, a lobbyist registry, pre-approved housing patterns, rental replacement and right of return, 99-year city-land leases, an emergency-access audit, a safety performance contract and community paramedicine. The campaign site links the Victoria City Hall Forum Facebook group, which McInnis administers, rather than a conventional candidate page. No official campaign account on Instagram, X, Bluesky, Threads, TikTok or YouTube was verified.

Sources. Campaign site and current platform, governance hub, 25 Jun launch coverage, professional biography and campaign-linked public posting history.

Current-platform note. The platform now contains materially stronger evidence than the mark counts below, especially explicit zero-based budgeting, a 90-day tax consultation, pattern-book housing, family targets, tenant protections, community paramedicine, infrastructure certification and multiple public dashboards. Existing grades remain unchanged until the 11 Sep lock; a complete crosswalk is required rather than selectively adding favourable items.

One genuine tension, unresolved and stated rather than smoothed. Infrastructure reviews tied to development approvals adds a condition to the approval path where M7 commits to compressing it. Both are accountability instruments; only one is a speed commitment. He has not published his reasoning and it should be asked rather than assumed. An earlier version of this page asserted a motive for it; that was inference and was removed.

📋 Correction history. Two claims about him on this page were this document's, not his: he is a former lawyer and educator, not a sitting UVic professor, and the reasoning attributed to him on infrastructure reviews is not on the record. On the tax number he was closer to the truth than the framework was — he said "10 per cent this year alone" and Victoria News corrected him to 9.34% residential, while the framework was leading with the 7.28% aggregate.
