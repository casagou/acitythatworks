Who they are

Marg Gardiner is a first-term Victoria councillor with a background in chemistry, public management, regulatory enforcement, programme management and data analysis. She holds a BSc in chemistry and an MBA in public management/administration, has lived in Victoria for more than two decades and served for 16 years on the James Bay Neighbourhood Association board before election. Her community work has covered public safety, land use, accessibility, transportation, parks, economic development and air quality. On council she has been one of the most consistent fiscal- and public-safety dissenters, opposing each annual tax increase and the Missing Middle policy. For voters, her campaign offers the clearest method-based alternative on budgeting and neighbourhood consultation, while her record on policing, supervised consumption and land use makes the substantive trade-offs unusually explicit.

2026 campaign and social verification. marggardiner.ca is now a current, authorized campaign site for City Council and CRD director with biography, priorities, voting record, endorsements, donations, volunteering, news, voter survey and campaign email sign-up. It is organized around four priorities: safe streets, liveable neighbourhoods, respect for the taxpayer and transparent governance. The site makes a direct zero-based-budget commitment, calls for every dollar to produce measurable results, supports police funding, opposes current Missing Middle/OCP density rules, advocates stronger community input and records her attempt to seek closure of The Harbour supervised-consumption site. Its statement that City reserves fell from about $300 million to $160 million is a campaign claim that was not independently reconciled in this pass and should not be quoted as a verified City statistic without checking reserve definitions. The current Facebook page is linked directly by the campaign; X identifies itself as her only campaign account. Two positions the framework has no measure for, and one live tension. She commits that "the City of Victoria must be prepared to support involuntary confinement for those who are a danger to themselves and others" — unscoreable, because the Program has no involuntary-care measure. She also commits that "the City must end financial support or tax exemption to social service programs which permit illicit drug use." That is not a gap in her platform, it is a direct tension with the Program: M65 states that its programme review is "not a viewpoint test," and M83 sets a viewpoint-neutral civic-access test of lawful use, safety and municipal purpose. M83 has no cell in the 55, so nothing is scored either way, but any reader comparing her to the Program on grants and tax exemptions will land on this, and the public card should not be the first place it is noticed.

The largest answerable gap: the CRD seat. She is seeking a CRD director seat alongside re-election, and has published no position on regional cost — nothing on the $1.07B Goldstream filtration plant, nothing on a per-household rate-impact statement, nothing on the City's annual position at the CRD table. A seat sought is not a source, so M70c stays a dash. It is the single question most worth putting to her: she is asking for the seat that decides the file.
