Background

Evidence vintage (summary). Verified 1 Sep 2026 against the new mayoral campaign site, current social profiles, 2023–2025 election coverage and the January 2026 Elections BC enforcement finding. The website contains a substantial high-level agenda, but not yet numerical targets, costings or a full measure-by-measure platform.

Web, campaign & socials (checked 1 Sep 2026). Campaign website · X · Facebook—personal/public page · Facebook—former Langford campaign · Instagram—former provincial campaign · YouTube—former Langford campaign

Profile. Mike Harris is a Vancouver Island businessman, housing and real-estate professional, former journeyman carpenter and repeat political candidate. His mayoral campaign describes more than 30 years in real estate, housing, construction and business; in a 2024 candidate questionnaire, he described more than 40 years as a businessman and entrepreneur. Harris Auto Group’s team page lists him as an owner. He presents himself as a practical operator focused on timelines, measurable results and value for taxpayers. He is also a husband, father and grandfather. For Victoria voters, his business experience is substantial, but so is the need to scrutinize how a private-sector management approach would translate to municipal law, collective agreements, regional governance and public-service delivery.

Residence and connection to Victoria. In a September 2024 Times Colonist questionnaire, Harris stated that he lived with his wife in the Happy Valley area of Langford and had done so for four years. The new mayoral site does not state a current City of Victoria residence or explain whether that has changed. Do not infer a move: verify his current residence and nomination documents after the City’s 11 Sep declaration of candidates. His business group has operated a Victoria-area dealership in Langford, and his campaign now proposes a 12-neighbourhood listening programme across Victoria.

Political and electoral history. Harris was the BC Conservative candidate in the June 2023 Langford–Juan de Fuca by-election and the October 2024 Langford–Highlands general election, finishing second to the NDP in both. In 2024 he received 8,038 votes, or 37.8%, according to the reported final result. He then sought the federal Conservative nomination in Cowichan–Malahat–Langford in early 2025. This history is relevant in Victoria’s formally non-partisan election: the 2026 mayoral website does not foreground a party label, but he has a recent and sustained Conservative campaign record.

The Conservative runs are sourced record, not an ACTW endorsement.

Published 2026 agenda. The site organizes Harris’s campaign around six priorities:

  1. Safe streets and strong communities — clean, accessible public spaces; compassion, coordination and accountability.
  2. Respect for taxpayers — review spending, reduce duplication and show residents what each tax dollar delivers.
  3. Housing that works — set measurable permit targets, reduce avoidable delays and judge policy by homes built and real affordability.
  4. Bring back the best of downtown — a measurable plan for a clean, safe, welcoming, accessible and active downtown.
  5. A fresh start at City Hall — an independent organizational and value-for-money review with public findings.
  6. 12 neighbourhoods, one Victoria — neighbourhood conversations followed by a public “What Victoria Told Mike” report.

Decision method. Harris proposes a six-question test before major spending: municipal responsibility, full cost, the problem being solved, success measures, less-expensive alternatives and whether affected people were consulted. The final test is “Did it work?” This is a clear governing frame; the missing piece is how it would be embedded in budgets, staff reports, procurement and quarterly public reporting.

📋 Campaign-compliance record. In January 2026, Elections BC fined Harris $450 because approximately 45,000 flyers from his 2024 provincial campaign lacked the required authorization statement. Elections BC found no deliberate attempt to circumvent the law, noted that the campaign cooperated and had no prior fines, and imposed a penalty well below the $10,000 maximum. Record this as an election-administration lapse with the mitigating findings, not as a policy score.

⚪ Still. Numerical tax and spending commitments; programme-by-programme savings; debt and reserve rules; a quarterly public dashboard; housing mix and non-market targets; tenant protections; police and bylaw staffing; homelessness pathways and service hours; transit and walking standards; climate adaptation; reconciliation and host-Nations relationships; CRD costs and votes; accessibility; major-capital referendum rules; municipal AI governance; and implementation costs and funding sources.

What would move it. A complete platform that converts the six priorities into numerical targets, baselines, costs, funding sources, responsible authorities and reporting dates. Also needed: confirmation of current residence, formal nomination status, a direct Victoria-focused interview, and clarification of whether the legacy Conservative campaign accounts will be converted to municipal campaign channels.
