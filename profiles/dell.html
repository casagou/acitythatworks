Background

Evidence vintage (summary). Verified 1 Sep 2026 against a refreshed 2026 campaign site and policy page, City biography, current social profiles, December 2025 re-election coverage and the four-year council record. The prior statement that no 2026 campaign site was located is obsolete.

Web, campaign & socials (checked 1 Sep 2026). Campaign site · Policies · X · Facebook—public page · Facebook—campaign/personal profile · Instagram · City biography

Profile. Matt Dell is a first-term Victoria councillor, provincial-government policy professional, former neighbourhood-association leader and longtime musician and arts organizer. He served five years as president of the South Jubilee Neighbourhood Association and has volunteered with National Little League and the Oaklands Parent Advisory Council. He holds a master’s degree in political science from the University of Victoria and lives in South Jubilee with his spouse and two children. First elected in 2022 with 13,899 votes—the third-highest total in a 37-candidate field—he has emphasized housing supply, public spaces, arts and culture, climate action and walkable neighbourhoods. For voters, the central assessment is delivery: his current campaign foregrounds projects and approvals from the term, while his voting record includes support for every annual budget and for the majority’s growth strategy.

2026 campaign and social verification. mattdell.ca is now a current, authorized re-election site with donations, volunteer recruitment, a record section and a linked policy programme. Its 2026 homepage highlights housing and affordability, a downtown public library, local business and tourism, completing Crystal Pool by 2030, parks and trees, streets and sidewalks, arts and events, and safe walkable neighbourhoods. The policy page expands this into eight themes: housing and affordability, climate, parks and amenities, neighbourhoods, arts and culture, downtown, governance and reconciliation. Much of the policy language is directional and the page is not visibly dated, so it should be treated as the programme linked by the 2026 campaign while individual carry-over promises remain subject to confirmation. X and both Facebook identities are active public channels; his campaign/personal Facebook profile reports a 2026 Victoria Labour Council endorsement. The Instagram identity is the one published in his 2022 materials, but a reliable current-post chronology was not available. No dedicated Bluesky, Threads or TikTok campaign account was clearly linked from the site.

Sources. 2026 campaign site, policy page, City biography, December 2025 re-election coverage, 2022 election results and council records. The site’s accomplishment totals are campaign-attributed until matched to City approval, completion and spending records.

No permit benchmarks, no dashboard, no ZBB position. His ❌ on managed competition was removed in v1.8 for having no source.

Fact-check of his comparative-tax claim, completed July 31 2026 — largely TRUE, and the wording matters. Dell's statement was that Victoria had "the lowest municipal tax increase of most surrounding major cities" between 2023 and 2025. Checked against the neighbouring municipalities: Victoria ran 6.15% / 7.93% / 6.99%. Saanich ran 7.19% / 7.93% / 8.02%. Esquimalt ran 6.3% / 7.72% / 9.90%. Oak Bay ran roughly 11.6% draft / \~9.7% / 11.4%. Langford ran 12.41% / 15.6% / 9.77%. Colwood ran 6.43% / 4.9% / below 5%. Verdict: cumulatively Victoria was lower than Saanich, Esquimalt, Oak Bay and Langford, and higher than the smaller Colwood. "Lower than most" is defensible and sourced. "Lowest of all" would be false, and he did not say it. Oak Bay's 2023 and 2024 finals and Colwood's 2025 final carry minor source discrepancies and are flagged in the Open Questions block. This is recorded because the framework's own accountability rule cuts both ways: a claim that survives checking should be recorded as surviving.
