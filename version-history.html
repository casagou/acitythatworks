Versioning principles

  • Snapshots preserved before substantive change. Each version is duplicated to a dated archive before the live master is edited.
  • Dated changelogs appended to the live master, not deleted. The history of why a measure changed lives next to the measure itself.
  • All prior versions remain accessible at their snapshot locations. Nothing is lost.
  • Major-version bumps (v1 → v2) are reserved for re-numbering and structural changes that would break existing cross-references.
  • Sub-version bumps (v1.1 → v1.2 → …) for content additions, sourcing corrections, expansions, and refinements that preserve numbering.
  • Anyone can contribute ideas, corrections, or new measures by writing to info@acitythatworks.ca. Every contribution is reviewed; accepted contributions are credited in the changelog.

Current version

v1.9.2July 31, 2026Live
The 12 Commitments scorecard, rebuilt

A fiscal commitment added to the accountability section for the first time. The framework's entire diagnostic opens on the budget, yet a voter who reached the scorecard could not find out what happens to their tax bill — Well-Managed and Democratic, two of five pillars, shared a single row between them. Row 1, residential tax rate, and row 2, verified annual savings, are new, and they are the two commitments no other candidate in the race can copy, because they require the arithmetic to have been done first.

Four rows replaced, each for a stated reason. Trees planted measured effort rather than result, so Measure 61's canopy percentage takes its place at row 12 alongside the GHG target; the planting count stays reported inside the measure itself. Stormwater upgrades carried a "replace oldest 20% of system" target that appeared nowhere in Measure 59; that work continues under Measure 59 and reports through the quarterly dashboard. Serious-injury collisions rested on a baseline of 1 to 3 fatalities a year — small enough that a 50% reduction is half a death, achievable by luck and missable by chance — and since Measure 40 was rewritten to advocacy the City no longer controls the main instrument. It is replaced by dangerous intersections redesigned: an output the City fully controls and can count. Green mobility is an existing OCP target rather than a new commitment, so it remains Measure 62 in full and is simply no longer one of the twelve headline numbers.

Two rows were also wrong rather than weak, and are corrected. Permit speed stated a flat −50% target when Measure 7's own category targets range from roughly 25% to 75%; row 8 now states simple, complex and rezoning targets directly. The intersections row had paired a serious-injury reduction target with a fatalities baseline — the exact mismatch that prompted its replacement above.

v1.9.1July 30, 2026
Reconciliation and correction release

Corrected the three-year growth figure to roughly 31% — $299,976,550 adopted in 2023 against $394,060,040 adopted for 2026, verified on the same envelope. Reconciled the headroom figures across every page to $4.3–14.0M central and −$0.3M pessimistic.

Rewrote Measure 40 as advocacy after confirming that BC's Intersection Safety Camera programme is not operated by, or delegated to, municipalities — the measure could not be delivered by the City as previously written, so it no longer claims to be.

The public "100 active commitments" framing is retired. Three earlier versions published a count that did not survive a hand count, so the framework now states no total; the 12 Commitments scorecard is the public-facing number.

v1.9July 30, 2026
Coordinated-independents absorption set

Eleven new sub-measures and three in-place strengthenings, for approximately $15–30K/year recurring and $175–290K one-time. New: M7b pattern-book housing, M8b suite-legalization amnesty, M26b civilian crisis response, M28d community paramedicine, M31c community-safety performance contract, M73d Strategic Economic Plan, M78b material change rule, M79d lobbyist registry, M79e FOI application-fee abolition, M79f remuneration deferral, M80c Local Area Plans. Strengthened in place: M7 tenure priority queue, M61 canopy target, M79 draft-back standard.

A further conditional tier of roughly $500–850K/year is excluded from every headroom figure: M26b, M28d and M80c. The first two proceed only with a confirmed provincial or health-authority cost-share partner, because municipally funding a clinical service outright is the downloading M70b exists to refuse; absent a partner both are advocacy at zero cost. Roughly two-thirds of v1.9's substantive value by dollar sits in that tier, which is the honest place for it.

This set was built to the post-v1.8 rule rather than around it. Every other item is cost-neutral or offset by name. Net headroom moves to approximately $4.27–13.99M central — inside the rounding of the v1.8 range rather than a movement of it. That is the design, not a coincidence: after v1.8 there was almost nothing left to spend.

v1.8.2July 30, 2026
Pre-publication consistency release

No measure added, no cost booked, no commitment changed. Nine internal contradictions found in a full-document review and closed. The two largest: The Method still opened with the superseded $384.1M / 29% / 10.44% diagnostic while the Well-Managed pillar carried the corrected one; and every fiscal rule was protected by a "supermajority of Council (6 of 8)" when Victoria's council has nine members — which Measure 79c stated correctly in the same document. Both fixed, the second in all six locations.

Also: the three-year growth figure is now 31% with both endpoints verified against the City's own adopted plans ($299,976,550 for 2023, $394,060,040 for 2026, same envelope, like for like); the Balance Sheet now leads with net headroom of $4.3–14.0M rather than the gross $12.5–27.8M; the unsourced 53% salary-share claim and the unsourced "lowest unemployment in the country" are removed; the canopy baseline is stated once; and both notes-to-self were resolved rather than deleted.

The 2027 question, answered from the plan. The adopted 2026–2030 Financial Plan contains no 2027 tax-increase percentage, so neither Gardiner's "roughly double" nor Dell's rebuttal was quoting a published figure. What the plan does show for 2027 is roughly $13.8M of new pressure against a $209.1M tax base — about 6.5 points before any wage, contract or police growth: the $2M debt-reduction contribution resuming, debt servicing up 32%, the capital transfer up $5.0M, and the parking contribution down $3.7M. Stated as arithmetic, not forecast.

Second pass, same day. Added a cost statement to all 35 measures that had none, every one $0, cross-referenced, or absorbed within an already-reconciled line. Added "Victoria in 2030". Reconciled the Savings & Revenue Analysis across 46 recurring, 9 capital and 8 one-time lines, with all 16 previously missing measures booked and net headroom restated at $4.3–14.0M central and about −$0.3M pessimistic. Brought the FAQ, One-Page Summary, Endorsement Pack and City Hall guide current. Added the CRD as a fourth row in the orders-of-government table. Resolved the tagline conflict in favour of "Proven ideas. Measurable results."

Two findings not on the defect list, disclosed rather than quietly fixed. First, "absorbed within" appeared 11 times with no running total anywhere; Savings Part C1 now totals every offset envelope, and the M68 attrition envelope carries $0.34–0.49M against a $1.9–4.0M saving, so 9% to 26% consumed. Second, Measure 26 and Measure 67 were absorbing costs from other measures while stating no budget of their own, so costs were charged to envelopes nobody could size. Both now costed explicitly — which moved the pessimistic case from roughly break-even to about −$0.3M. Worse-looking, more honest, correct direction.

v1.8.1July 30, 2026
The cap decision

Measure 66 now caps the residential property tax rate, not the total levy. Council raised total 2026 revenue 7.28% and distributed it at 9.34% residential / 4.78% business, so a cap on the total would have permitted a 9% homeowner bill described as 6.5%. Added a ratio rule preventing the business-to-residential split being moved to manufacture the number, an assessment caveat, and a commitment to publish both figures annually with the dollar change on a median home.

The Year 1 claim got stronger by getting more honest — 6.5% is a three-quarter-point reduction against the aggregate and nearly three points against the residential rate. Three factual corrections from the City's adopted figures: operating budget $394.1M adopted (not the $384.1M draft); opening draft 13.3% (not 10.44%); and the residential split now shown for two years.

v1.8July 30, 2026
Questionnaire gap closure

Eight new sub-measures written against the categories Victoria candidates will actually be questioned on, and the gaps neither this framework nor the incumbent field addressed: M13d standing School District 61 agreement, M20b a walking city, M23b transit control and funding, M50b arts and culture funding floor, M53d municipal AI standard, M66d the Household Bill, M70c the regional bill and Victoria's CRD representation, M82b turnout and ballot access.

Five in-place strengthenings and one correction of an internal contradiction: M24 committed the City to street-level computer vision while the framework committed to no City cameras. Booked cost −$110–200K/yr with named offsets; three further commitments worth $450–700K/yr were deliberately gated on verified Year 1 savings rather than booked, because the pessimistic headroom case had no room in it. The public "100 active commitments" framing is retired.

How to read the history

Each release above lists what changed, why, and where, with measure numbers. Cross-bloc attributions are tagged, so anyone who proposed an idea first can find their contribution credited. The dated changelogs for every step are appended to the working master alongside the measures themselves, and archive snapshots preserve the verbatim state of the framework at v1.1, v1.2, v1.3.3, v1.4, v1.5 and v1.7.1.

◆ Why the changelogs are not on this site
The 14 appended changelogs run to roughly 11,400 words. They are the record of why each measure changed, and they belong next to the measures on the working master rather than in front of a voter. This page is the summary of all 14; the snapshots hold the verbatim history. Nothing exists in only one place.
