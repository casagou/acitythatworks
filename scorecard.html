◆ Read this before you read the grades

Silence never lowers a grade. A candidate with no located position on a topic is excluded from the average rather than marked down — it narrows the base the grade is computed from, which is why every cell shows n of total. A B+ on 3 topics is a narrower claim than a B on 23, and the numbers are there so you can tell.

A C is not a bad grade. All-partial computes to exactly C. It means the candidate has said something concrete without naming an instrument — the ordinary state of a municipal platform in July, before platforms are published.

A low grade almost always means “we could not find a published mechanism,” not “this candidate is wrong.” Candidates: if we have missed something you published, write to info@acitythatworks.ca and the grade is corrected and dated. Several already have been, in candidates’ favour.

How a grade is computed

Each of the 53 topics carries a mark for every candidate, and a mark requires a citable public source: a recorded council vote, a published platform, or a direct quotation in a named outlet. A grade is the mean of the scored topics in that area.

✅Aligned — names the same mechanism ACTW proposes +3
🟢Close — a different mechanism doing substantially the same work +2
🟡Partial — a concrete commitment in the area, mechanism differs or unspecified +1
❌Opposed — a recorded vote against, or an explicit statement of opposition −3
⚪No public position — nothing located excluded
📋Record — a fact about a term in office, not a stated position excluded
⛔No information — nothing about the candidate could be found at all excluded

All-aligned = A. All-close = B+. All-partial = C.
