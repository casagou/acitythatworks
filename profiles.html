◆ Read this before the profiles

A profile is not a verdict. The grade measures distance from this framework, not quality, and a narrow evidence base is a fact about a publication calendar rather than about a candidate. Most challengers publish detail in September; a re-score after nominations close 11 September is mandatory.

Every grade here is current. Some of the prose is not. Marks excluded on 1 August 2026 are still described as marks in a few profiles — open for the full list, and which way each correction ran

These profiles were written against the 31 July evidence base. On 1 August 2026 a full mark-by-mark re-verification applied one rule harder than before: A characterisation is not a citable sentence. Fifteen marks rested on tone or posture rather than on something a candidate said or voted for, and were excluded. Several profiles below still describe those positions as marks. Fact-check of his comparative-tax claim, completed July 31 2026 — largely TRUE, and the wording matters. Dell's statement was that Victoria had "the lowest municipal tax increase of most surrounding major cities" between 2023 and 2025. Checked against the neighbouring municipalities: Victoria ran 6.15% / 7.93% / 6.99%. Saanich ran 7.19% / 7.93% / 8.02%. Esquimalt ran 6.3% / 7.72% / 9.90%. Oak Bay ran roughly 11.6% draft / ~9.7% / 11.4%. Langford ran 12.41% / 15.6% / 9.77%. Colwood ran 6.43% / 4.9% / below 5%. Verdict: cumulatively Victoria was lower than Saanich, Esquimalt, Oak Bay and Langford, and higher than the smaller Colwood. "Lower than most" is defensible and sourced. "Lowest of all" would be false, and he did not say it. Oak Bay's 2023 and 2024 finals and Colwood's 2025 final carry minor source discrepancies and are flagged in the Open Questions block. This is recorded because the framework's own accountability rule cuts both ways: a claim that survives checking should be recorded as surviving. ---
