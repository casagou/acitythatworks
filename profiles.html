◆ Read this before the profiles

A profile is not a verdict. The grade measures distance from this framework, not quality, and a narrow evidence base is a fact about a publication calendar rather than about a candidate. Most challengers publish detail in September; a re-score after nominations close 11 September is mandatory.

Every grade here is current. Some of the prose is not. Marks excluded on 1 August 2026 are still described as marks in a few profiles — open for the full list, and which way each correction ran

These profiles were written against the 31 July evidence base. On 1 August 2026 a full mark-by-mark re-verification applied one rule harder than before: A characterisation is not a citable sentence. Fifteen marks rested on tone or posture rather than on something a candidate said or voted for, and were excluded. Several profiles below still describe those positions as marks. They are listed here so nothing is quietly deleted:

  • Pro-reconciliation M1–M5 — Caradonna · Dell · Thompson · Kim · Loughton
  • Pro-transit / pro-bike M19/M22/M38–M42b — Caradonna · Dell
  • Province-blaming present in framing M70/M70b — Dell · Thompson · Loughton
  • Climate leadership stated M58/M58b/M64b — Caradonna · Dell · Kim — the cooling bylaw is already scored once at M9
  • Pro-heritage tone M43–M46b — Gardiner
  • Recorded pro-enforcement votes, unnamed M27 — Gardiner — the qualifying vote is already scored once at M33

Three marks were regraded upward: the CSWB adoption vote of 3 July 2025 carried Commits at M26 and M27 but Agrees at M11 for the same councillors on the same act. It is now Commits at all three (Dell, Kim and Coleman at M11, Weak → Partial).

Every excluded mark was worth +0.5, below almost every mean, so the correction ran upward. The defect had been quietly suppressing sitting councillors' grades: Caradonna and Dell each moved up one letter. Each excluded mark is restorable the moment a vote or a quotation is attached to it — write to info@acitythatworks.ca.

📋 Accountability — what the council did, on the record. Four consecutive years above inflation: 6.15%, 7.93%, 6.99%, and 7.28% aggregate in 2026, which is 9.34% residential and 4.78% business, about $323 more on a median $1,015,000 home. The 2026 budget opened at 13.3% in November and roughly $7M of the reduction came from one-time measures including a cancelled $2M debt-reduction payment and a $2.8M parking-reserve draw — an approach she described herself as not a long-term solution, which is the exact conduct M66c is written to prevent. Zero modern efficiency tools in four years: no ZBB, no competitive benchmarking, no permit-processing benchmarks, no quarterly dashboard, no municipal AI standard. CSWB Phase 1 funded by drawing $2.5M from Centennial Square, $4M from the Financial Stability Reserve and $1.5M from Royal Athletic Park. Dowler Place: a confidential draft motion authored by her, to convert Dowler Place into a Pandora relocation site, was disclosed by CHEK via an FOI in which the documents were accidentally attached, June 2025, with consultation skipped because "we knew what the comments would be from the public." She issued a statement: "I recognize that there has been confusion and frustration… residents haven't had a full or accurate picture of the project… I apologize for that confusion." A related item belongs on the record and was missing from this page: firefighter Josh Montgomery was suspended one day for an open letter to Premier Eby opposing the hub; Alto declined to apologise to him and publicly told the Premier that local personnel matters were "not his bailiwick" (Times Colonist, 2024). She has stated no position against any of these measures. This is what a council did across four budgets, and it is the baseline any 2027 council starts from.

⚪ Still. Permits and AI pre-screening, managed competition, the performance dashboard, accessibility and washrooms, streetlighting, GIS and AI governance, the Integrity Commissioner, the regional bill, the Household Bill.

What would move it. A published position on any of the five method measures. Four years of a mayoralty produce marks; the method gap is a record, and only a stated commitment closes it. ---

Full profile →

Decision 14 overall letter D · 5 scored answers. See the scorecard →

Web & socials. mattdell.ca · X · FB

Sources. No dedicated 2026 site located; Victoria News Dec 31 2025, Victoria Buzz Jan 5 2026, CHEK 2025. 15 years drafting policy for the BC government. 13,899 votes in 2022 in a 37-candidate field.

📋 Accountability. YES on every budget; "between 2023-2025 the City of Victoria had the lowest municipal tax increase of most surrounding major cities," a comparative frame rather than a restraint commitment. No permit benchmarks, no dashboard, no ZBB position. His ❌ on managed competition was removed in v1.8 for having no source.

Fact-check of his comparative-tax claim, completed July 31 2026 — largely TRUE, and the wording matters. Dell's statement was that Victoria had "the lowest municipal tax increase of most surrounding major cities" between 2023 and 2025. Checked against the neighbouring municipalities: Victoria ran 6.15% / 7.93% / 6.99%. Saanich ran 7.19% / 7.93% / 8.02%. Esquimalt ran 6.3% / 7.72% / 9.90%. Oak Bay ran roughly 11.6% draft / ~9.7% / 11.4%. Langford ran 12.41% / 15.6% / 9.77%. Colwood ran 6.43% / 4.9% / below 5%. Verdict: cumulatively Victoria was lower than Saanich, Esquimalt, Oak Bay and Langford, and higher than the smaller Colwood. "Lower than most" is defensible and sourced. "Lowest of all" would be false, and he did not say it. Oak Bay's 2023 and 2024 finals and Colwood's 2025 final carry minor source discrepancies and are flagged in the Open Questions block. This is recorded because the framework's own accountability rule cuts both ways: a claim that survives checking should be recorded as surviving. ---
