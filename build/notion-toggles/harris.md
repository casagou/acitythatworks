**Profile.** Mike Harris is a Vancouver Island businessman, housing and real-estate professional, former journeyman carpenter and repeat political candidate. His mayoral campaign describes more than 30 years in real estate, housing, construction and business; in a 2024 candidate questionnaire, he described more than 40 years as a businessman and entrepreneur. Harris Auto Group’s team page lists him as an owner. He presents himself as a practical operator focused on timelines, measurable results and value for taxpayers. He is also a husband, father and grandfather. For Victoria voters, his business experience is substantial, but so is the need to scrutinize how a private-sector management approach would translate to municipal law, collective agreements, regional governance and public-service delivery.
**Residence and connection to Victoria.** In a September 2024 *Times Colonist* questionnaire, Harris stated that he lived with his wife in the Happy Valley area of Langford and had done so for four years. **Political and electoral history.** Harris was the BC Conservative candidate in the June 2023 Langford–Juan de Fuca by-election and the October 2024 Langford–Highlands general election, finishing second to the NDP in both. In 2024 he received 8,038 votes, or 37.8%, according to the reported final result. He then sought the federal Conservative nomination in Cowichan–Malahat–Langford in early 2025. This history is relevant in Victoria’s formally non-partisan election: the 2026 mayoral website does not foreground a party label, but he has a recent and sustained Conservative campaign record.

**Published 2026 agenda.** The site organizes Harris’s campaign around six priorities:
1. **Safe streets and strong communities** — clean, accessible public spaces; compassion, coordination and accountability.
2. **Respect for taxpayers** — review spending, reduce duplication and show residents what each tax dollar delivers.
3. **Housing that works** — set measurable permit targets, reduce avoidable delays and judge policy by homes built and real affordability.
4. **Bring back the best of downtown** — a measurable plan for a clean, safe, welcoming, accessible and active downtown.
5. **A fresh start at City Hall** — an independent organizational and value-for-money review with public findings.
6. **12 neighbourhoods, one Victoria** — neighbourhood conversations followed by a public “What Victoria Told Mike” report.
**Decision method.** Harris proposes a six-question test before major spending: municipal responsibility, full cost, the problem being solved, success measures, less-expensive alternatives and whether affected people were consulted. The final test is “Did it work?” This is a clear governing frame; the missing piece is how it would be embedded in budgets, staff reports, procurement and quarterly public reporting.
**🟢 Close (2).** **Published permit-performance commitment:** measurable permit targets and judging policy by homes built, close to M7 but without a numeric service standard, quarterly publication or AI pre-screening. **Independent organizational and value-for-money review:** a concrete public review close to M65, but without ACTW’s rotating zero-based cycle, three-part programme test or savings target.
**🟡 Partial (5).** Safety and clean public spaces, without enforcement hours, command structure, staffing or funding (M14/M26/M27/M33). Respect for taxpayers, without a tax glide path, debt rule or Household Bill (M65/M66/M66c/M66d). A measurable downtown plan, without vacancy, licensing or programme targets (M71). Twelve neighbourhood conversations and a public report, without a permanent participation or reporting mechanism (M80). Housing affordability and faster delivery, without a unit-composition target, non-market share or tenant-protection position (M6/M9).
