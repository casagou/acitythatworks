**Profile.** Marianne Alto is Victoria’s incumbent mayor and is seeking a second term. A professional facilitator with university degrees in law and science, she owns Azimuth Research & Consulting, specializing in problem solving, change management and facilitation, and lives in Burnside Gorge. First elected to Victoria City Council in 2010, she served 12 years as a councillor before winning the mayoralty in 2022 with 15,090 votes (about 55%). She also served on the Capital Regional District Board from 2011–2018 and returned in 2022. Her current regional roles include the CRD Board, BC Transit Board and BC Urban Mayors’ Caucus; she has chaired the CRD Arts Commission and Performing Arts Facilities Select Committee. She has also held long-standing City Family and reconciliation roles with the Songhees and Esquimalt Nations. For voters, the central fact is experience: by the 2026 election she has 16 years at the council table, including four as mayor, so her candidacy can be assessed against an extensive municipal and regional record.

**2026 campaign status and priorities.** Alto formally launched her re-election campaign on 20 May 2026 under the slogan “Experience that delivers.” Her three announced priorities are **unlocking more homes**, **taking action on community safety**, and **investing in community and wellbeing**. She has also named transportation, parks, business support, arts and culture, heritage and regional policing as second-term priorities. She says she will not run with a council slate.
**Housing evidence — approvals are not completions.** Her launch cited more than 7,000 housing units approved during the term. The *Times Colonist* separately reported 2,359 net new occupancy permits since provincial targets began in 2023—about 48% of the five-year target of 4,902—and 363 below-market rentals over the preceding two years. These figures measure different stages and should not be conflated when assessing delivery.
**Safety and wellbeing evidence.** Alto presents the Community Safety and Wellbeing Plan as unfinished multi-year work requiring continued funding and intergovernmental partnerships. Launch coverage credited the programme with 97 new living spaces and increased police, bylaw, sanitation and service-provider capacity; treat those as campaign claims pending a published outcome dashboard.
