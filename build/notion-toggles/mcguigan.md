**Profile.** Bruce McGuigan is a sociologist, former Vancouver Island University professor, former senior government policy advisor, non-profit executive and public-policy consultant making a second run for mayor. His career has combined education, regulation and enforcement policy, community services and social innovation. He led Family Services of Greater Victoria for five years, co-founded the Victoria Social Innovation Centre and, according to his public professional profile, served briefly as acting executive director of the Victoria Immigrant and Refugee Centre Society in late 2025. His education includes a BA (Honours) in political sociology from McGill University and an MA in sociology from the University of Calgary. For voters, his strongest case is management and public-policy experience across government and community organizations; the key test is whether he converts that experience and a values-heavy platform into costed, municipal mechanisms that a mayor can deliver.
**Victoria connection and electoral history.** A 2018 report described McGuigan as a longtime Fernwood resident, and his current campaign states that he lives and works on the homelands of the lək̓ʷəŋən peoples. He first ran for Victoria mayor in 2018, placing fourth with 2,377 votes, or 8.0%, behind Lisa Helps, Stephen Hammond and Michael Geoghegan. He did not hold elected office as a result. CHEK reported his renewed candidacy on 20 Aug 2026, describing him as a former professor and current consultant. His current platform combines progressive social and pro-union commitments with fiscal-accountability, performance-management and local-business themes.

**Guiding principles.** The campaign frames municipal leadership around three principles: **listen carefully**; **manage competently** by setting goals, assigning responsibility and measuring results; and **use public resources responsibly** by identifying costs, intended outcomes and value received. These are cross-cutting management commitments, not yet a published operating system or budget process.
**Published 2026 priorities.**
1. **Ending homelessness** — make homelessness rare, brief and non-recurring; coordinate permanent housing, health care, treatment and recovery; prevent discharge from public systems into homelessness; and work with regional, provincial, Indigenous and lived-experience partners.
2. **Local business and entrepreneurship** — address safety, cleanliness, transportation, infrastructure and employee housing, and establish a City-backed Buy Local campaign.
3. **A citizens-first City Hall** — open decisions, understandable budgets, transparent contracts, early and continuous consultation, and a public account of how input affected decisions.
4. **Fair governance and public accountability** — publish straightforward accounting for spending, public land, beneficiaries, foregone revenue and public value.
5. **Listening to workers** — a pro-union commitment to living wages, secure employment, apprenticeships, strong public services and worker participation.
6. **Housing everyone** — market and non-market supply, non-profit and supportive housing, complex-care housing, family-sized rentals, affordable ownership and protection of lower-cost rentals; use public land for lasting value and consider displacement alongside supply.
7. **Safe streets for everyone** — appropriate policing and enforcement, prevention, public-space design, late-night safety and civilian or health responses where appropriate, with conduct rather than housing status as the focus.
8. **Climate preparedness and resilience** — building efficiency, extreme heat, wildfire smoke, resilient facilities, water management, food security, transit and active transportation.
9. **Community wealth and a strong local economy** — support businesses, workers, co-operatives, social enterprises, Indigenous businesses and non-profits, using procurement and investment to retain prosperity locally.
10. **Creativity, arts and innovation** — strengthen the conditions for artists, cultural organizations, entrepreneurs and innovators to live and work in Victoria.
